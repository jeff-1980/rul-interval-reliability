Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Reliability Failure of Remaining-Useful-Life Prediction Intervals under Sensor Degradation: A Leakage-Audited Evaluation, Mean-Scale Attribution, and Maintenance-Decision Consequences," for publication in IEEE Transactions on Reliability as a regular paper.

Prediction intervals on remaining useful life are consumed by maintenance decisions, so their reliability is a question of when they fail and what the failure costs, not only of whether they are calibrated on clean data. The paper evaluates five interval mechanisms on two backbones and all four C-MAPSS sub-datasets under an engine-level protocol with disjoint training, validation and calibration sets, and reports four results that survive checks across backbones, decomposition order and independent noise realisations:

(i) test-set checkpoint selection and normaliser scope move RMSE by 10-18% and the NASA score by 16-113% on the same architecture, with a non-additive interaction;

(ii) coverage lost under additive sensor noise is lost predominantly through the mean prediction, robustly to decomposition order (16/16 point estimates, 14/16 paired intervals), with no order-independent evidence that the learned scale ever helps;

(iii) structured degradations fail on opposite sides of a maintenance rule: within-window drift makes the models pessimistic and triggers premature maintenance on 65-73% of six-condition engines holding over 100 cycles of true life, whereas bias produces unrecognised maintenance needs;

(iv) neither coverage nor a range-based input-excursion statistic characterises these risks, and cost rankings between mechanisms reverse across cost ratios.

The contribution is methodological and empirical rather than algorithmic; no new architecture is proposed. Every table is generated by script from released result files, and two independent runs of the released code reproduce the 31 result files behind the tables bit-for-bit on the stated environment (https://github.com/jeff-1980/paper2, tag v0.9-review, REPRODUCE.md). Supplementary material provides per-table provenance, full four-way attribution values, the complete decision stress test, and latency distributions.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no conflict of interest and received no specific funding for this work.
